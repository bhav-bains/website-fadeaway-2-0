Your website is your best salesperson. On Black Friday it pulls a double shift: every shopper at once, at 2 a.m. and at noon, and no second chance with the ones who leave. Get it ready now. Once the sale starts, there's no time to retrain it.

It's written for store owners, e-commerce managers and marketing teams on any platform: Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Adobe Commerce (Magento), Wix, Squarespace or a custom build. Most of these checks take minutes.

## When should you start getting your store ready?

The order here is important. Fix what matters while there's still time, then put the tools down a week before the sale.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 4 weeks out (early November) | Run the checks below. Write down every problem and sort it into "I can fix this" and "needs a developer." Book developer time now, before everyone else does. |
| 2 to 3 weeks out (by mid-November) | Make the fixes. Build your Black Friday offer page. Test every fix on real devices and screen sizes: phones, foldables, tablets, laptops and desktops. |
| The week before (November 20 to 26) | Stop making changes. No new apps, no theme updates. Place a real test order from start to finish. Schedule your emails and posts. Confirm stock levels and shipping cutoffs. |
| During the sale (November 27 to 30) | Watch, don't rebuild. Each morning, check that orders are coming in, pages load, payments go through and sold-out items are marked. |
| After the sale (December) | Look at what sold, where shoppers dropped off and what broke. Keep your shipping cutoff dates visible for December shoppers. Plan bigger fixes for January and the first quarter of next year. |

In Canada, Boxing Day (December 26) is a second rush. The same checklist applies, so the work you do now pays off twice.

## The checklist

Start at the top. The first five groups move sales this month. The other four still count; some of them just pay off later, in December and into next year.

### 1. Site performance

Shoppers are impatient on a normal day. On Black Friday, with a dozen tabs open and a sale in every one, they're worse. A slow page loses them before your offer even shows up, on a phone, a laptop or anything in between.

- Open your analytics (Tech details) and see which devices your shoppers use most: phone, desktop or tablet. Test hardest on the ones that bring in the most sales.
- Open your homepage, a product page and your checkout on your phone (on mobile data) and on a laptop. Notice how long each one takes.
- Run your top pages through [Google PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) (free). Look at both the mobile and the desktop score. Fix first for the device most of your shoppers use.
- Shrink oversized images. Large photos are the most common reason a store feels slow.
- Remove apps and scripts you no longer use. Each one adds weight to every page.
- Check every pop-up on your phone and laptop. Each one should work properly and make shopping easier, not harder: easy to read, easy to close, and never covering the product or the buy button.

### 2. Product pages

Think of each product page as the salesperson standing by the shelf. If a shopper has to go hunting for an answer, it should be easily findable on the page.

- Clear photos from more than one angle.
- Price, sale price and what's included are easy to see.
- Sizes, colors and stock status are correct.
- Shipping cost and delivery time are on the page, not hidden until checkout.
- Your return policy is linked from the page.
- Reviews are visible, if you have them.
- The products you're pushing for the sale are one tap away from the homepage and menu.

### 3. Cart and checkout

This is the part that pays the bills. A surprise shipping fee, a form that won't take a postal code, one step too many, and the cart just sits there. Full and abandoned.

- Place a test order on your phone from start to finish with a real card, then refund it.
- Offer guest checkout, so nobody has to create an account to buy.
- Turn on the express payment options your platform supports, such as Apple Pay, Google Pay or Shop Pay.
- Test every discount code: right amount, right products, right dates.
- Show the full cost, with shipping and taxes, before the last step.
- Make sure order confirmation emails send and look right.
- Your abandoned cart email is switched on, and you've tested it by leaving something in a cart yourself.

### 4. Your offer and sale page

If a shopper has to read your offer twice, it's too complicated.

- One clear offer, stated in one sentence.
- One sale page or collection that your homepage, emails and ads all point to.
- Start and end dates on the page.
- The sale banner works on a phone and doesn't cover the products.

### 5. Tracking

Broken tracking doesn't announce itself. You find out in December, staring at a report that says your best weekend of the year came from nowhere.

- Google Analytics records purchases with revenue. Check that your test order shows up. Google's [guide to measuring ecommerce](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/ecommerce) covers the purchase event.
- Your ad pixels (Meta, Google Ads, TikTok) fire on the order confirmation page.
- Every email and ad link has tracking tags, so you can see which ones brought sales.
- Write down your normal daily sales now, so you have something to compare against.

### 6. Site structure, search and AI visibility

Search is slow. Google can take days or weeks to pick up a change, and AI answers like ChatGPT can take longer, so most of this pays off in December and next year. Check it now anyway. A broken sitemap the week before the sale is not a fun surprise.

- Your main collections and your sale page are one or two clicks from the menu, and every product links back to its collection. A clear structure helps shoppers and search engines find what matters.
- In Google Search Console, open the Pages report. Look at why any product or collection pages aren't indexed, and fix the ones that should be.
- Your sitemap is submitted in Search Console. When your sale page goes live, paste its address into the URL Inspection tool and request indexing.
- Every product page has its own title and description, not the platform default.
- Product details like price and availability are marked up in structured data, so Google and AI assistants can read them. Most platforms do this, but themes and apps can break it. Google explains what it reads in its [product structured data guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product).
- Sold-out products stay up with a "notify me" option instead of being deleted. A deleted page loses everything it built in search.
- Your site doesn't block search engines or AI crawlers from your product pages.
- Use the same web address for your sale page every year (for example /black-friday/). After the sale, keep the page live with a short "see you next year" note instead of deleting it, so it keeps the links and search history it built.

### 7. Google Merchant Center

If your products show up in Google Shopping, free listings or Shopping ads, your product feed has to be right before the sale. Google checks the feed against your site, and a mismatch can pull products out of Shopping right when you need them most.

- Check Merchant Center for disapproved products or account warnings, and fix those first.
- Prices and sale prices in the feed match your site. Give sale prices a start and end date so they switch on and off on time.
- Stock in the feed matches your store, so sold-out items stop showing.
- Shipping and return settings in Merchant Center match your store's policies.
- Not set up yet? Review can take a few days, so either start this week or leave it for January.

### 8. Shipping, returns and policies

- Holiday shipping cutoff dates are on product pages, in the cart and in your FAQ.
- Your return window for holiday gifts is clear.
- Your contact details and reply times are easy to find.
- Gift options, like gift notes or gift receipts, work if you offer them.

### 9. Stock, apps and capacity

- Stock counts are right, and sold-out items can't be ordered.
- Every app you rely on during the sale is updated before you stop making changes, not during.
- If your store isn't on a hosted platform like Shopify, ask your host whether your plan can handle a traffic spike.
- Someone is on call during the sale to answer customers and fix problems.

## What not to touch during the rush

The safest store on Black Friday is the one you already tested. Mid-sale problems tend to start with a small change that looked harmless at 9 a.m.

**Always keep a backup.** Before you stop making changes, back up your whole store: theme, products, pages and settings, plus the site files and database if you host it yourself. Keep that copy until the sale is over, and take a fresh one before any fix you make mid-sale. If something breaks, you can roll back instead of rebuilding under pressure.

- No theme updates or redesigns.
- No new apps, and no app updates unless something is broken.
- No new payment or shipping settings.
- No changes to web addresses, menus or page structure.
- Price and stock edits are fine. That's what the sale needs.

If something does break, fix only that, test it on a phone, and write down what you changed.

## After the rush

The week after Cyber Monday is when your store has the most to tell you. You won't have this many real shoppers in your numbers again until next year.

- Compare sales with the normal day you wrote down. Which products, emails and ads brought the most?
- Look at where shoppers dropped off: product page, cart or checkout.
- List everything that broke or slowed down, and what you did about it.
- Keep shipping cutoffs and gift return details visible for December shoppers.
- Set up automations that keep selling after the sale: a thank-you email after the first order, a review request once the order arrives, back-in-stock alerts for anything that sold out, and a reminder for shoppers who left something in their cart.
- Tag your Black Friday first-time buyers, so you can welcome them properly and invite them back in the new year.

Then sort the list. Small fixes can go in before Boxing Day or the December rush. Bigger ones, like a new theme, a platform move or a rebuilt checkout, belong in the first quarter of the new year, when there's time to do them properly.
